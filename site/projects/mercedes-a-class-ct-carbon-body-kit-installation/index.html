When a Mercedes Doesn't Need Reinventing – It Needs Refining

Some vehicles benefit from dramatic modification.

Others lose everything that made them special the moment the first aftermarket part is installed.

The Mercedes-Benz W177 A-Class belongs firmly in the second category.

Since its launch, the latest generation A-Class has become one of the most recognisable premium hatchbacks on the road. With its sweeping shoulder lines, aggressive front fascia, sculpted rear bumper and unmistakable Mercedes design language, it already possesses the proportions enthusiasts spend thousands of pounds trying to recreate.

That's precisely why modifying one requires a different mindset.

At FX Detailing, we don't believe great vehicle enhancement is about adding as many parts as possible.

It's about understanding the original design, respecting the manufacturer's intentions and carefully selecting modifications that elevate the vehicle rather than overpower it.

Every curve, every line and every panel on a Mercedes has been shaped through thousands of hours of engineering, aerodynamic testing and design refinement. A successful body kit shouldn't compete with that work — it should complement it.

That philosophy guided every stage of this project.

The owner of this Mountain Grey Mercedes A-Class approached us with a clear objective.

They weren't looking to create a loud show car.

They weren't trying to imitate an A45 AMG.

Instead, they wanted something considerably more timeless.

A refined OEM+ transformation that would sharpen the vehicle's appearance while remaining unmistakably Mercedes.

To achieve that, we installed a complete CT Carbon exterior styling package consisting of a front splitter, front canards, rear diffuser and roof spoiler.

The result is subtle enough that many people would assume it left the factory this way.

Yet parked beside a standard W177, the difference becomes immediately obvious.

The vehicle sits lower.

It appears wider.

The body has more presence.

Every angle feels more purposeful.

This is exactly what thoughtful modification should achieve.

Not attention for the sake of attention.

Just better design.

The Mercedes-Benz W177 – Why It Became One of the Best Platforms for OEM+ Styling

When Mercedes-Benz introduced the fourth-generation A-Class in 2018, it marked one of the biggest design evolutions in the model's history.

Previous generations had always been premium hatchbacks.

The W177 became something more.

Mercedes completely reimagined the proportions of the car, giving it a lower roofline, a wider stance and significantly more aggressive body sculpting. Even before AMG variants entered production, the standard car already carried a level of visual confidence normally associated with much larger performance vehicles.

Unlike many modern hatchbacks, whose styling can feel overly complicated, the W177 strikes an exceptional balance between elegance and aggression.

The long shoulder line stretches uninterrupted from the headlights to the rear lamps.

The front bumper incorporates deep character lines that naturally direct airflow around the vehicle.

The rear bumper has been sculpted to create visual width, while the roofline gently tapers into a subtle factory spoiler.

Every element feels cohesive.

Every panel works with the next.

From a designer's perspective, the W177 is already remarkably complete.

That presents an interesting challenge for modification specialists.

Adding parts purely for the sake of adding them almost always makes the vehicle look worse.

The objective shouldn't be to redesign Mercedes' work.

It should be to enhance what's already there.

This is exactly why the W177 has become one of the most popular platforms for OEM+ styling.

Rather than requiring dramatic changes, relatively small aerodynamic enhancements can completely alter the character of the vehicle.

A carefully designed front splitter changes the way the front bumper sits visually.

A roof spoiler extends the entire silhouette.

A deeper rear diffuser introduces width and aggression without overwhelming the factory design.

The result is transformation through refinement rather than reinvention.

For many enthusiasts, that's exactly what modern modification should represent.

What Does OEM+ Actually Mean?

You'll often hear the term OEM+ used within the performance car community.

Unfortunately, it's also one of the most misunderstood phrases in automotive culture.

Some assume it simply means fitting genuine manufacturer accessories.

Others believe it refers to subtle modifications.

In reality, it's far more considered than that.

OEM+ is a philosophy.

It asks a simple question.

"If Mercedes had one more year to perfect this vehicle, what might they have changed?"

The answer rarely involves oversized wings or exaggerated styling.

Instead, OEM+ focuses on enhancing proportions.

Improving balance.

Adding definition.

Refining details.

The goal is to create a vehicle that appears completely natural.

One that even seasoned enthusiasts could mistake for a factory special edition.

That's exactly what attracted this customer to CT Carbon.

Rather than attempting to transform the Mercedes into something it isn't, every component has been designed to work with the existing bodywork.

The front splitter extends the bumper without appearing oversized.

The canards sharpen the outer edges without becoming visually distracting.

The diffuser introduces aggression while maintaining the original proportions of the rear bumper.

The roof spoiler lengthens the silhouette without dominating it.

Individually, each component appears relatively subtle.

Together, they completely change the character of the vehicle.

This is why OEM+ styling has become increasingly popular among owners of premium vehicles.

It's timeless.

Unlike trend-driven modifications that quickly date a car, OEM+ enhancements tend to age remarkably well because they're rooted in the manufacturer's original design language.

Five years from now, this Mercedes will still look sophisticated.

That's the difference between following trends and respecting design.

Inside Our Installation Process

Every body kit installation follows a carefully structured process developed through years of working with premium and performance vehicles.

Rather than rushing components onto the vehicle, we work methodically through each stage to ensure every part integrates perfectly with the factory bodywork.

Step One — Initial Inspection

Before any work begins, every component is individually unpacked and thoroughly inspected.

This allows us to assess the quality of each part before installation while checking for consistency in finish, manufacturing accuracy and overall condition.

Any issues can be identified immediately rather than halfway through the project.

Step Two — Vehicle Preparation

Preparation is one of the most overlooked stages of any installation.

Every mounting surface must be perfectly clean before permanent fitting begins.

Road contamination, old protection products, polishing oils and general surface residue can all reduce adhesive performance over time.

We therefore spend considerable time preparing the vehicle before a single component is installed.

It's a stage customers rarely see, but it's absolutely essential for long-term durability.

Step Three — Dry Fitting

This is arguably the most important stage of the entire installation.

Every component is temporarily positioned on the vehicle without permanent fixing.

This allows us to evaluate:

  • Panel alignment
  • Symmetry
  • Body line continuity
  • Overall proportions
  • Visual balance

Small adjustments made during this stage produce dramatic improvements in the finished result.

Step Four — Precision Installation

Only once we're completely satisfied with the positioning do we proceed with permanent installation.

Working systematically ensures every component sits exactly as intended while maintaining consistency across the entire vehicle.

There is no rushing this stage.

Precision always takes priority over speed.

Step Five — Final Quality Inspection

Once installation has been completed, every component undergoes a comprehensive visual inspection.

We assess the vehicle from multiple viewing angles, checking body line continuity, symmetry and overall presentation.

Only once we're completely satisfied does the Mercedes leave the workshop.

Because that's ultimately what our customers are investing in.

Not simply new parts.

Confidence that they've been installed to the highest possible standard.

Common Mistakes We See During Body Kit Installations

Unfortunately, we regularly see vehicles arrive after unsuccessful installations carried out elsewhere.

Sometimes customers simply aren't happy with the finish.

In other cases, poorly fitted components have started lifting, shifting or developing uneven gaps only weeks after installation.

Although every project is different, the same mistakes tend to appear time and time again.

Poor Surface Preparation

Preparation is the foundation of every successful installation.

If mounting surfaces aren't properly cleaned and prepared before fitting, adhesives cannot achieve maximum bond strength.

The result is often premature lifting, especially around splitters and spoilers that are exposed to high airflow.

At FX Detailing, thorough preparation is never treated as an optional extra.

It's an essential part of the installation process.

Rushing the Alignment

Many installations fail because components are permanently fixed before their positioning has been properly evaluated.

Even slight misalignment becomes immediately noticeable once viewed from different angles.

That's why we dedicate significant time to dry fitting every component before final installation begins.

Taking longer at this stage prevents disappointment later.

For owners who take pride in keeping their vehicles looking their absolute best, ceramic coating is one of the most worthwhile investments that can be made following any exterior modification.

Mercedes A-Class Body Kit Buyer's Guide

Choosing the right body kit isn't simply about selecting the most aggressive design.

It's about finding components that complement both your vehicle and the way you use it.

Here are some of the questions we discuss most frequently with Mercedes owners considering exterior upgrades.

Should I Install Everything at Once?

Not necessarily.

Many owners begin with a front splitter before gradually adding matching components over time.

As long as the parts belong to the same styling package, the vehicle can evolve naturally without compromising its overall balance.

Is Gloss Black or Carbon Fibre the Better Choice?

This ultimately comes down to personal preference.

Gloss black offers a clean, understated appearance, while carbon fibre introduces greater visual depth and texture.

On lighter coloured vehicles such as Mountain Grey, carbon fibre creates a particularly striking contrast without appearing excessive.

Will a Body Kit Affect Everyday Driving?

Quality components designed specifically for the W177 platform should not compromise everyday usability when installed correctly.

However, front splitters naturally reduce front ground clearance slightly, meaning additional care should be taken when approaching steep driveways or speed bumps.

Should I Protect New Components Immediately?

Absolutely.

Protecting newly installed components from day one is considerably easier than restoring neglected finishes later.

This is one of the main reasons so many customers choose to have their vehicle ceramic coated immediately after installation.

Can I Upgrade the Car in Stages?

Yes.

Many of our customers begin with one or two components before returning to complete the full package at a later date.

Because CT Carbon products are designed as a cohesive range, they integrate beautifully regardless of whether they're installed together or progressively over time.
